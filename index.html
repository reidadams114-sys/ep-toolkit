Become the best in the world at follow up and next steps
📈 Pain + Reflection = Progress
An accurate understanding of reality is the essential foundation for producing good outcomes.
Have clear goals
Identify and don’t tolerate problems that stand in the way of goals
Accurately diagnose those problems
Design plans that explicitly lay out tasks that will get you around your problems
Implement these plans — do the tasks

🕊 Leading While Grieving
Acknowledge the loss (loss of my self-confidence)
Accept the gift of mourning
Adjust your expectations. When you or your team are in a season of loss, it’s not the time to add a lot — personally or in the ministry
Embrace the waves of grief and productivity. It comes and goes
Communicate to your team, family, and friends
It’s never time for perfectionism, but especially not when there is grieving happening
Build your replenishment cycle: Spiritual (the state of my inner world) · Physical (moving my body, nutrition) · Intellectual · Emotional · Social (friends and community)
✨ Principles of Excellence
The aggregation of marginal gains — 1% improvement in everything we do
Excellence is not about radical changes, but about accruing small improvements over time
Time will magnify whatever you feed it
If you are struggling to improve, the problem isn’t you — it’s your systems
You do not rise to the level of your goals; you fall to the level of your systems
The more things we chase, the slower progress will be

👥 Hiring Philosophy
Hire people who:
Work for what they want and not what others want of them
Come up with the best independent opinions they can muster to move toward their goal
Stress-test opinions by having the smartest people they can find challenge them
Are wary of over-confidence, and good at not knowing
Wrestle with reality, experiencing the results of their decisions and reflecting on what they did to produce them so that they can improve
24 hours a day, 365 days a year, you should be able to answer the following questions: what are the key seats in my organization? what % of those seats can I say with confidence are filled with the right people? what are my plans for increasing that %? what are my backup plans if a right person leaves a key seat? — Jim Collins
It’s pretty easy to spot an overwhelmingly strong candidate or even an overwhelmingly weak one. It’s the whelming candidate you must avoid at all costs, because that’s the one who can and will do your organization the most long-lasting harm. Overwhelmers earn you raves. Underwhelmers leave on their own or are terminated. Whelmers, sadly, are like a stubborn stain you can’t get out of the carpet. They infuse an organization and its staff with mediocrity; they’re comfortable, and so they never leave; and frustratingly, they never do anything that rises to the level of getting them promoted or sinks to the level of getting them fired. And because you either can’t or don’t fire them, you and they conspire to send a dangerous message to your staff and guests that average is acceptable. — Danny Meyer
Without the right people in the right seats in the right roles with the right perspective in the right environment, effective and efficient progress will be elusive and any progress will cost exponentially more than it should.
